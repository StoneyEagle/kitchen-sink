# Inline formatting

<!-- block: 01KPMN045RP644N62SPX1NRXQB -->
Regular prose is what most of an entry looks like. Sentences go here without any decoration, and that is the happy path most readers see.

<!-- block: 01KPMN045R8JD7HKDQCEBRQG5K -->
You can emphasise a word with **bold** or *italic*, and the two compose into ***bold italic*** when you need to be emphatic about emphasis. Inline `code` stays monospace and breaks out of the reading font so the reader's eye catches it. Links like [Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com) open in a new tab by convention.

<!-- block: 01KPMN045RENM1RZFNJYBNKSZD -->
Strikethrough via `~~text~~` becomes ~~rendered text~~ — useful for showing edits, corrections, or crossed-out mistakes without removing the original content. The TTS narrator skips struck-through content by default so listeners hear the intended reading, not the redaction.

<!-- block: 01KPMN045R220CRQH8H4TR0BQ9 -->
Abbreviations unlock accessible narration. The acronym <abbr title="Cascading Style Sheets">CSS</abbr> is read as "cascading style sheets" when the text-to-speech narrator encounters it, not as three spelled-out letters. <abbr title="Accessible Rich Internet Applications">ARIA</abbr> and <abbr title="Cross-Origin Resource Sharing">CORS</abbr> behave the same way.
